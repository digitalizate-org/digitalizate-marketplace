## Why
Alan Cooper identifies three distinct goal types that must all be satisfied: Experience Goals (how users want to feel: competent, in control, not stupid), End Goals (what users want to accomplish: find the report, approve the request), and Life Goals (who users want to be: a trusted analyst, a productive professional). Enterprise software consistently addresses only End Goals, creating functional but emotionally unsatisfying tools. Designing for all three creates products that users choose to use rather than endure. Experience Goals in particular drive the emotional quality of the product and long-term user loyalty.

## How to implement
- For each primary persona, write explicit statements for all three goal types before any design work begins.
- Derive Experience Goals directly from research — ask users how they want to feel at the end of a successful workday, not just what they want to accomplish.
- Use Experience Goals as quality criteria during design critique alongside functional acceptance criteria.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Error counts
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, Ease-of-use score, NPS
  **How to measure**: Use post-task surveys in usability testing to assess whether users feel the product served their broader life and end goals, not just functional tasks. Compare NPS scores before and after redesigns grounded in this goal framework.

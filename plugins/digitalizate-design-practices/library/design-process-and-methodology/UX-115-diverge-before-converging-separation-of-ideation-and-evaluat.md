## Why
Generate the broadest possible range of ideas before evaluating any of them. IDEO's core ideation principle requires explicitly separating divergent thinking (generating possibilities) from convergent thinking (evaluating and selecting). Teams that evaluate while generating consistently produce fewer and less creative ideas. For enterprise software, this combats the common tendency to jump immediately to "the obvious solution" — which often means a slightly modified version of the existing system rather than a genuinely better approach.

## How to implement
- Run a structured brainstorm: 10–15 minutes of silent individual idea generation (no talking, everyone writes/sketches), then share-outs round-robin, then "yes-and" building on others' ideas.
- Explicitly defer all judgment and critique until the ideation period ends — anyone who starts evaluating during ideation gets a gentle redirect.
- After 20–30 minutes of divergence, switch to convergent dot voting to identify the most promising directions.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS, Ease-of-use score
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
  **How to measure**: Compare the number of distinct solution concepts generated per ideation session when diverge/converge separation is used vs. when it is not. Track downstream usability test success rates for designs produced with structured diverge-before-converge ideation to assess whether broader exploration yields higher-quality solutions.

## Why
Traditional design starts with the homepage and works down, leaving the most important content — the "core" users seek — as an afterthought. Designing from the core outward ensures all inward paths lead efficiently to the most valuable content, and outward paths from the core reflect business priorities. This approach avoids the common failure of homepage-centric design that prioritizes company structure over user destination.

## How to implement
- Identify the core offering first (what users are actually trying to get to or do).
- Map all inward paths to the core (search, navigation, direct links).
- Map all outward paths from the core (next steps, related actions, conversion points).
- Design the homepage and navigation last — after core and paths are defined.
- Rank outward paths by business importance to prioritize visual prominence.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Completion rate, Task success rate
- **Efficiency**: Time on task, Completion time
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, Satisfaction rating
**How to measure**: Evaluate designs produced using Cores and Paths against task completion rates in usability testing, specifically measuring whether users can reach and act on core content without unnecessary steps. Track time-on-task across key user journeys compared to designs that did not start from the core content outward.

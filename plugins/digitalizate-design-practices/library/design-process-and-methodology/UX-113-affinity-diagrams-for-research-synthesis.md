## Why
Cluster qualitative research findings — interview quotes, observation notes, pain points — according to intuitive relationships (similarity, theme, dependency) to discover connections and reveal innovation opportunities. Affinity diagrams transform hundreds of raw observations into actionable insight clusters. They are especially valuable for cross-functional teams where multiple researchers need to build a shared understanding of diverse findings. The unexpected clusters that don't fit neatly into anticipated themes often represent the most valuable insights.

## How to implement
- Write each research observation on a separate sticky note (physical or digital).
- Have team members arrange silently by affinity without discussing.
- Name clusters with insight statements, not category labels ("Users work around the approval step" not "Approval process").
- Look for the unexpected — clusters that contradict assumptions are the most valuable.
- Photograph or digitize the final affinity map.
- Convert the top 5–10 insight clusters into "How Might We" questions for ideation.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Efficiency**: Training time, Time saved per task
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, CSAT
**How to measure**: Compare the time required to synthesize research insights and identify themes using affinity diagrams versus unstructured review; track how frequently synthesized insights translate into actionable design decisions and whether teams report higher confidence in findings.

## Why
In enterprise software, executives are the buyers but employees are the users. Executives have a bird's-eye view of processes — they don't know how operations look from inside the tools. Collecting feedback only from decision-makers produces designs that satisfy purchase criteria but fail day-to-day usability needs. The people who experience the UI's friction daily are the users — their input is irreplaceable and cannot be approximated by executive summaries.

## How to implement
- Define user roles and core users before designing (analyst, manager, coordinator, administrator).
- Interview employees directly about their tasks, pain points, and workarounds.
- Conduct observations in the actual work environment to capture real workflows.
- Run usability tests with actual end-users performing real tasks, not executives reviewing mockups.
- Iterate designs based on end-user feedback before executive review.
- Use role-based personas grounded in field research, not assumed user archetypes.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Error counts
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS, Ease-of-use score
  **How to measure**: Compare usability test success rates on products designed with end-user research vs. executive-only input. Track support ticket volume as a proxy for unmet real-user needs after design changes.

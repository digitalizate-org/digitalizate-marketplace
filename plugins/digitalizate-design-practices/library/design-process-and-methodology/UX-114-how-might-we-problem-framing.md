## Why
Synthesize research insights into focused, human-centered problem statements using the "How Might We" (HMW) format. HMW questions are optimally sized: broad enough to allow creative solutions but narrow enough to provide meaningful direction. The format is human-centered (starts with the user's need), not solution-biased (avoids presupposing a specific answer), and inherently actionable. For complex enterprise products, good HMW questions reframe "add a feature" requests into underlying user needs that may be addressable in multiple ways.

## How to implement
- Generate HMW questions directly from pain points in research data (quote or observation → HMW question).
- Format: "How might we [verb + user benefit]?" Avoid HMW questions that imply a solution ("HMW add a filter?" implies filters are the answer; "HMW help users find the right data quickly?" is better).
- Generate 10–20 HMW questions from a research set.
- Vote on the most promising using dot voting.
- Use the selected HMW as the ideation brief.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS, Ease-of-use score
  **How to measure**: Compare the ratio of problem-reframing to solution-jumping in design sessions and track whether HMW-grounded designs yield higher usability test success rates. Use post-launch satisfaction surveys to assess whether HMW-guided solutions better match real user needs.

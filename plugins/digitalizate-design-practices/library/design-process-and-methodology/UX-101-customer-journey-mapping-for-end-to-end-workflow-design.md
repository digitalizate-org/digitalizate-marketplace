## Why
Map the complete sequence of user interactions, emotional states, and touchpoints across an end-to-end workflow. Journey maps reveal pain points and missed opportunities invisible when examining individual screens in isolation. For enterprise/B2B tools, journey maps must include pre-application work (gathering data, consulting colleagues), in-application workflows, and post-application actions (reporting, handoffs), because value frequently breaks down at these boundary conditions rather than within the application itself.

## How to implement
- Map workflow stages across the horizontal axis, user actions/thoughts/feelings along the vertical.
- Capture: what the user does, what they think, what they feel, and what system touchpoints they interact with at each stage.
- Highlight "moments of truth" — steps where failure causes maximum user harm or frustration.
- Identify pain points at workflow boundaries (entering the tool, leaving the tool, handoffs to other people) which are often the highest-value improvement opportunities.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: NPS, CSAT, Ease-of-use score
**How to measure**: Baseline CSAT and task completion rates at each mapped touchpoint, then re-measure after workflow redesign informed by the journey map; track support ticket volume to validate reduction in pain-point-driven contacts.

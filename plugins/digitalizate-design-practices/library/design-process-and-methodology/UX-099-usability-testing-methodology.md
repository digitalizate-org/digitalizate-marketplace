## Why
Moderated usability testing — watching real users complete realistic tasks with a prototype or live product — identifies problems that heuristic evaluation misses, including domain-specific workflow issues, mental model mismatches, and context-of-use problems. As few as 5 participants in a homogeneous user group reveals approximately 85% of critical usability issues, making this a high-ROI research method. The think-aloud protocol — asking users to narrate their thoughts and actions — provides rich qualitative data that reveals the why behind observed behavior.

## How to implement
- Define 3–5 tasks representing the most critical user journeys.
- Recruit 5–8 participants matching your actual user profile (role, domain expertise, typical workflow context).
- Use think-aloud protocol: "Please narrate what you're thinking as you work." Take detailed notes on hesitations, errors, backtracking, and confusion points.
- Analyze for themes across participants, not isolated incidents.
- Follow heuristic evaluation with usability testing — they are complementary, not substitutes.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate, Error rate
- **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, SUS score, CSAT
**How to measure**: Establish a pre-launch usability test benchmark (task success rate, time on task, SUS score) and track improvements across iterative test rounds. Compare pre- and post-redesign support ticket volume to confirm that usability testing directly reduces user errors and help requests in production.

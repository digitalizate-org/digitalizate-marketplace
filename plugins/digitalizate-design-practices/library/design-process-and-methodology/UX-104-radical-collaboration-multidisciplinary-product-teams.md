## Why
IDEO's design thinking depends on "radical collaboration" — bringing together designers, engineers, business strategists, domain experts, and customers as co-creators rather than sequential contributors. Multidisciplinary teams catch more risks earlier, generate more creative solutions, and build shared ownership of outcomes. For enterprise software, this means product designers working directly with data engineers, domain SMEs, and end users — not receiving requirements through a chain of intermediaries. Sequential waterfall handoffs between discovery, design, and development are the primary source of expensive late-stage rework.

## How to implement
- Involve at least one representative from design, engineering, business/product, and a user-facing role (customer success, sales, or actual users) in all discovery and design phases.
- Establish shared artifacts — journey maps, personas, IA diagrams — that all roles co-create and reference throughout the project.
- Schedule weekly cross-functional reviews rather than phase-gate handoffs.
- Measure collaboration health by whether engineers understand why they're building what they're building.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: SUS score, NPS
**How to measure**: Baseline SUS and NPS scores before and after implementing collaborative design processes across release cycles; track support ticket volume as a proxy for product-quality improvements over time.

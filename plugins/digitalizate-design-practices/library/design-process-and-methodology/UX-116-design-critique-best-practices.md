## Why
Design critiques are structured feedback sessions that improve design quality iteratively. Effective critiques require: clear objectives (what specific feedback is needed?), relevant stakeholders (not too many people), pre-review materials shared in advance, structured feedback (critique the design against its stated goals, not personal preference), and clear ownership (the designer makes the final decision). Without this structure, critiques become subjective opinion battles where the most senior voice dominates, producing designs that satisfy organizational politics rather than users.

## How to implement
- Share pre-review materials (video walkthrough or annotated mocks) 24 hours before the meeting.
- Open the critique with: "The design goal is [X]. We need feedback on [specific questions]." Separate problem validation from solution feedback.
- Require feedback to be framed as observations or questions, not directives ("I notice users might be confused by X" rather than "Change X to Y").
- Document decisions and rationale in a shared artifact.
- Timebox sessions at 60–90 minutes maximum.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Error rate
- **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, Ease-of-use score
**How to measure**: Track usability test scores (task success rate, SUS) across product releases and correlate with the adoption of structured critique practices. Monitor support ticket volume post-launch as a signal of whether critique-driven quality improvements reduce user errors in production.

## Why
B2B and enterprise journey maps focus on business outcomes across roles and departments, not individual personas. Enterprise systems support mission-critical functions with system-dictated parameters — journey maps must empower users within those constraints while identifying friction points across the full process. A journey map covering only one user's portion misses the systemic nature of enterprise workflow problems. Cross-reference maps with analytics data to identify where stagnation or drop-off occurs.

## How to implement
- Map the full business process from start to completion, not just one user's portion.
- Include multiple roles and their interactions at each touchpoint.
- Capture thoughts, actions, and emotions at each stage to reveal friction points.
- Use journey maps to assign departmental ownership for each touchpoint.
- Cross-reference with analytics data to identify where stagnation or drop-off occurs.
- Use journey maps as a stakeholder alignment tool — they make cross-departmental friction visible to decision-makers.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS
**How to measure**: Use journey maps to identify the highest-friction process touchpoints, then measure task success rate and time on task for those specific steps before and after redesign. Track support ticket volume for processes mapped in the journey to quantify the reduction in operational pain points.

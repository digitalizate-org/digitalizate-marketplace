## Why
The "Shallow Dive" approach uses deliberately rough, low-fidelity wireframes to surface critical design questions early — before effort is invested in high-fidelity detail. Fidelity of prototypes should reflect their stage of refinement; early explorations should be rough by design to encourage iteration rather than defense of polished solutions. Using rough wireframes to facilitate discussion about content structure and priority prevents stakeholders from engaging with layout decisions prematurely.

## How to implement
- Begin with intentionally rough wireframe fragments targeting specific design questions (hierarchy, flow, information priority).
- Use rough sketches to extract key decisions from stakeholders early while changes are cheap.
- Gradually increase fidelity as decisions are made and validated.
- Use low-fidelity wireframes to discuss content structure and priority — not visual design decisions.
- Reserve high-fidelity work for validated design directions only.
- Annotate wireframes with explicit design questions to focus review discussion.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, Ease-of-use score
  **How to measure**: Track design iteration cycles and the number of significant structural changes made after high-fidelity prototyping begins; compare projects that used early lo-fi wireframes vs. those that jumped to high-fidelity. Measure usability test success rates at the same project phase for both approaches as a quality proxy.

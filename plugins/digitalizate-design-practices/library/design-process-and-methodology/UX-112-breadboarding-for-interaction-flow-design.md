## Why
Use text-based breadboard sketches — listing Places (screens), Affordances (buttons/fields/links), and Connections (navigation flows triggered by affordances) — to define the topology of an interface without getting trapped in visual details. Breadboards allow rapid iteration over interface logic and navigation structure using words rather than pictures, enabling teams to evaluate three different interaction approaches in the time it would take to sketch one wireframe. They are especially valuable for discovering whether a navigation model is coherent before visual design investment.

## How to implement
- Draw three columns: Places (underlined, representing screens or locations), Affordances (listed below each place, representing buttons, links, and fields), Connections (arrows between places triggered by affordances).
- Use words only — no boxes, no visual styling.
- Work alone or with one trusted partner.
- Complete a breadboard in 15–30 minutes.
- When a breadboard feels overly complex or confusing as text, that complexity is a design problem to solve before proceeding.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Error counts
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, CSAT
  **How to measure**: Compare usability test success rates on flows designed with breadboarding vs. those designed directly in high-fidelity. Track design iteration cycles as a proxy for whether early flow clarity reduces rework and downstream usability errors.

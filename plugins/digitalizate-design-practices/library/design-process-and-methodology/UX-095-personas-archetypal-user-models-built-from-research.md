## Why
Create 1–3 primary personas representing archetypal users based on qualitative research, with explicit goals, behaviors, aptitudes, environments, and pain points. Personas prevent designing for yourself, resist feature accumulation by keeping design focused on real needs, and provide a shared language for cross-functional decision-making. For enterprise software, effective personas must capture the specific role context, domain vocabulary, and workflow environment of users — not just demographic information. A weak persona is a name with a stock photo; a strong persona is a decision tool that can answer "would Sarah use this?"

## How to implement
- Build personas from qualitative research (contextual interviews, observation).
- Each primary persona needs: name, role, key goals (all three types), specific workflow context, domain expertise level, key pain points, and a representative verbatim quote.
- Validate all persona claims against actual research data.
- Post personas visibly in team workspace and reference them explicitly in design reviews and feature debates.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Efficiency**: Training time, Time saved per task
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, CSAT
**How to measure**: Track how often design decisions reference persona data throughout the project; measure alignment between designed features and stated user needs by conducting post-launch usability tests with users matching persona archetypes and comparing success rates.

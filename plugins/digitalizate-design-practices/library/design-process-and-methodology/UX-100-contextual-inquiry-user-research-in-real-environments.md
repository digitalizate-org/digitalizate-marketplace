## Why
Conduct user research in context — at the user's workstation, in their actual workflow environment — rather than in sterile conference rooms. Contextual inquiry blends traditional interview questions with real-time task observation, allowing researchers to follow up immediately on behaviors they witness. This is the highest-yield research method for enterprise software because it captures both explicit needs and the tacit knowledge workers have built up over time — workarounds, reference materials, parallel tools, and interruption patterns invisible in interview-only research.

## How to implement
- Recruit 6–10 users per key persona.
- Use a semi-structured guide with open-ended questions.
- Ask users to narrate their actions while performing real tasks (not demo tasks — real in-progress work).
- Record with consent.
- Look for reference materials on their desk, other applications open simultaneously, and workarounds to your product.
- Debrief within 24 hours while observations are fresh.
- Include "Fly on the Wall" observation periods where you observe without asking questions.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Error rate
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Training time
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, CSAT
  **How to measure**: Run moderated usability tests before and after design iterations informed by contextual inquiry to measure task success and time-on-task improvements. Track support ticket volume as a downstream indicator of real-environment usability gaps.

## Why
Create a structured visualization of what users Think, Feel, Say, and Do to build a holistic understanding of their experience. Empathy maps surface the gap between users' external behaviors and internal emotional states — revealing frustrations users don't articulate and motivations that drive real behavior. For enterprise software, empathy maps often reveal that users "say" they want more features while they "feel" overwhelmed by complexity already present — a critical signal for prioritization. The Say/Think gap is often where the most valuable design insight lives.

## How to implement
- Use a 2×2 grid or a segmented circle: Says (direct quotes from research) / Thinks (inferred thoughts and internal narrative) / Does (observed behaviors and actions) / Feels (emotional states and reactions).
- Add "Pains" and "Gains" sections at the bottom.
- Populate with direct research data — quotes for Says, observations for Does, inferences supported by evidence for Thinks and Feels.
- Build one empathy map per primary persona.
- Use as a reference artifact in design reviews when debating how users will respond to a design decision.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, Ease-of-use score, NPS
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
  **How to measure**: Compare design iteration quality (measured by usability test success rates) on projects that used empathy mapping in synthesis vs. those that did not. Survey team members on confidence in design decisions as a proxy for research synthesis quality.

## Why
IA defines the information backbone of the system; navigation is the UI surface that exposes it. Starting with navigation forces the IA to conform to visual constraints rather than actual content complexity. A content inventory and audit must precede wireframing — this reveals what content exists, its relationships, and user-centric organization patterns. Navigation design decisions made without IA work are costly to reverse after engineering begins.

## How to implement
- Conduct a content inventory before defining navigation structure.
- Perform a content audit to evaluate usefulness, accuracy, and effectiveness.
- Document IA in spreadsheets and diagrams — not wireframes or prototypes.
- Group information user-centrically based on user needs, not organizational structure or database architecture.
- Develop a controlled vocabulary (taxonomy) applied consistently across all site content.
- Use card sorting to validate groupings with real users before committing to structure.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Findability score, Task success rate
- **Efficiency**: Time on task, Tree-test score
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, SUS score
**How to measure**: Run tree testing before and after restructuring the IA to measure direct and indirect success rates; supplement with moderated usability sessions to capture time-on-task and subjective ease-of-use scores.

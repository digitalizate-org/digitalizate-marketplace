## Why
Dopamine is released during the anticipation of a reward more powerfully than during the reward itself. Unpredictable, variable rewards — discovering unexpected useful information, finding a new shortcut, receiving surprising positive feedback — generate stronger engagement than predictable, fixed rewards. For enterprise software, this means proactively surfaced insights ("Your processing time is 40% faster than last month"), discovery-oriented information architecture, and recommendation features create genuine, authentic engagement — not through artificial gamification but through legitimate information value.

## How to implement
- Design "discovery moments" — places where the system proactively surfaces valuable, unexpected information.
- These are most effective when: occasionally surfaced (not every session), genuinely valuable and data-driven (not self-promotional), and personally relevant (based on actual usage patterns).
- Avoid making these moments predictable (e.g., "Did you know?" that appears every Monday) — variability is a core feature of their effectiveness.
- Reserve them for genuinely valuable insights.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Engagement & Usage**: Sessions per user, Active users, Feature usage
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS, Ease-of-use score
  - **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Renewed subscriptions
  **How to measure**: Track feature usage rates for customization and preference controls via analytics. Use post-task surveys to measure perceived control and satisfaction, and compare retention rates between user cohorts who actively personalize their experience vs. those who do not.

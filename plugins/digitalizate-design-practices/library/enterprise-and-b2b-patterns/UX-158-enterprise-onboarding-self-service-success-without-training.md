## Why
"A little bit of training is required because it's complex" is an excuse that undermines adoption. Enterprise onboarding must orient users, deliver immediate value, and create confidence in the first session — without external training. The gap between consumer and enterprise UX has narrowed dramatically: teams and employees now choose their own tools, making UX quality a direct adoption driver. Aim for advocacy-ready onboarding: users confident enough to show the product to teammates after one session.

## How to implement
- Collect key context upfront (role, industry, team size) and use it to customize the initial experience.
- Automate initial configuration steps that can be inferred from context — reduce "blank slate" anxiety.
- Use in-context tips and empty state messaging rather than forced tutorial flows.
- Measure success by whether new users can complete their primary task in the first session without external help.
- Aim for advocacy-ready onboarding: users confident enough to show the product to teammates after one session.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Efficiency**: Training time, Support tickets reduction
- **Revenue**: Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), Cost reduction
- **Adoption & Retention**: Account creation rate, Returning visitors
**How to measure**: Track support ticket volume and training time per new user before and after rollout; measure onboarding completion rate and time-to-first-key-action segmented by cohort in product analytics.

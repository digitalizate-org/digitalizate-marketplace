## Why
Plain language is the practice of writing content that any reader can understand on first reading without specialist knowledge. Research and government content design guidelines ([GOV.UK](http://gov.uk/)) demonstrate that plain language directly reduces cognitive load, increases task completion rates, and improves inclusivity for users with lower literacy levels, cognitive disabilities, or who are reading in a second language. [GOV.UK](http://gov.uk/)'s principle is that content should be readable by a 9-year-old using the 5,000 most common English words. Beyond inclusion, plain language enhances cognitive fluency — familiarity with word choice translates directly into faster comprehension and higher trust.

## How to implement
- Use short, common words: 'help' not 'assist', 'buy' not 'purchase', 'use' not 'utilize', 'show' not 'demonstrate'
- Write sentences of 15–20 words maximum for body text; under 10 words for critical instructions and error messages
- Use active voice: 'Click Save to continue' not 'Completion of saving is required before continuation'
- Eliminate buzzwords and marketing language ('synergize', 'leverage', 'best-in-class') that add words without adding meaning
- Explain technical terms on first use; better yet, replace them with plain alternatives where possible
- Test content readability using Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level tools; target grade 6–8 for general audiences, grade 4–6 for high-volume transactional content
- Review all error messages, notification copy, and instructional text for jargon before launch — technical teams often write for themselves, not for users

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, CSAT
- **Adoption & Retention**: Signup completion rate
**How to measure**: Run comprehension tests on key interface labels and instructions before and after plain-language rewrites; track support ticket themes related to terminology confusion and measure drop in tickets after copy simplification.

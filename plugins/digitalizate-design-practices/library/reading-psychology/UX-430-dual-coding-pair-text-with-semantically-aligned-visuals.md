## Why
Dual coding theory (Allan Paivio, 1971) proposes that the brain has two separate but interconnected cognitive systems: a verbal system for processing language and a non-verbal system for processing images. When information is encoded through both channels simultaneously, the brain creates multiple retrieval routes, dramatically improving comprehension and retention. The critical requirement is semantic alignment — the image must express the same concept as the text, not merely decorate it. Misaligned visuals create cognitive interference and reduce comprehension. When text and visuals are aligned and co-located, comprehension can improve by 40–79%.

## How to implement
- For every complex concept, process, or relationship in body text, evaluate whether a diagram, illustration, icon, or chart could communicate the same idea through the visual channel
- Place visuals within 8–16px of the text they illustrate — never separate them to different sections of the page
- Present visual and verbal content simultaneously (not sequentially) when possible; avoid carousels that show images separate from their captions
- Ensure visuals are semantically congruent with their adjacent text: a diagram showing a workflow must match the steps described in the text exactly
- Use icons next to navigation labels and category headings to encode meaning in both channels simultaneously
- Avoid purely decorative stock images adjacent to important copy — they consume visual attention without adding meaning and may actually reduce comprehension by occupying cognitive resources
- For instructional content (tutorials, onboarding), use step-by-step annotated screenshots rather than generic product images

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Efficiency**: Time on task
- **Engagement & Usage**: Scroll depth, Bounce rate
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, Satisfaction rating
**How to measure**: Run comprehension tests measuring accuracy and speed when users interpret text-only versus dual-coded (text + aligned visual) content; track scroll depth and time-on-page to confirm that paired visuals improve engagement with instructional or informational content.

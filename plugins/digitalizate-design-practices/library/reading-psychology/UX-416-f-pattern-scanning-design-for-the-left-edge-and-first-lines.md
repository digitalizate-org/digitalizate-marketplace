## Why
Eye-tracking research by the Nielsen Norman Group confirms that users reading unformatted web content follow an F-shaped pattern: a horizontal scan across the top, a shorter horizontal scan below, then a vertical scan down the left edge. The right side of pages routinely receives little attention, and critical information buried in the middle or right of body text is often skipped entirely. This pattern emerges because users are efficient, not lazy — they maximize information gained per unit of time. F-pattern scanning is especially prevalent when content appears as undifferentiated walls of text with no clear visual structure.

## How to implement
- Place the most critical message or call-to-action within the first two paragraphs where top horizontal scans are concentrated
- Front-load each paragraph: put the key idea in the first sentence, supporting detail after
- Never bury essential information (pricing, deadlines, key actions) in the right half of body text columns
- Use the left-edge vertical scan to your advantage: start list items, subheadings, and paragraph lead sentences with the most information-rich words
- Break up wall-of-text layouts with subheadings, bullets, and visual groupings to disrupt pure F-pattern scanning and promote richer engagement
- On mobile, recognize that the entire width is a 'left edge' — ensure full-width content is structured with strong opening sentences

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Completion time
  - **Engagement & Usage**: Scroll depth, Bounce rate, Pages per session
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, CSAT
  **How to measure**: Use eye-tracking studies to verify F-pattern scanning behavior on text-heavy pages and confirm that critical information and CTAs are placed in the high-attention left edge and first lines. Measure time-to-find-information and compare task completion times for layouts designed vs. not designed for F-pattern reading.

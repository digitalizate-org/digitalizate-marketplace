## Why
On pages with sparse text and a strong visual layout — such as landing pages, hero sections, and marketing layouts — users follow a Z-shaped path: left-to-right across the top, diagonally down to the lower-left, then right across the bottom. This differs from the F-pattern which applies to text-heavy content. Understanding which pattern applies depends on content density and page type. Designers can deliberately place key elements (logo, headline, CTA, social proof) at the four corners and diagonal midpoint of the Z to align with this natural eye path.

## How to implement
- On hero sections and landing pages, place the brand/logo at top-left, primary headline at top-right or center-top
- Position a supporting image or value proposition along the diagonal midpoint
- Place the primary call-to-action (CTA) button at the bottom-right — the natural termination point of the Z
- On cards and grid layouts, put the most important element (product name, price, badge) at the top-left of each card
- Avoid placing critical information in the center-middle where the diagonal scan crosses: this area receives the least dwell time
- Use visual weight (size, color, contrast) at Z anchor points to confirm the intended reading order

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Efficiency**: Time on task
- **Revenue**: Conversion rate, Clickthrough rate (as Revenue driver)
- **Engagement & Usage**: Clickthrough rate
**How to measure**: Use eye-tracking or heatmap tools to verify attention follows the Z-pattern on low-text pages such as landing pages; A/B test Z-pattern layouts against alternative arrangements and measure CTA click rate and conversion rate.

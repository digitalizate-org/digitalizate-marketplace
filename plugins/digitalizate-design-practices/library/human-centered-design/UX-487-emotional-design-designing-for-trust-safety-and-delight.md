## Why
IDEO's approach to product design explicitly addresses emotional experience as foundational, not supplemental. David Kelley's 8 Design Abilities include 'communicating deliberately' — understanding that design must resonate emotionally, not just function correctly. Drawing on Don Norman's three levels of emotional design (Visceral, Behavioral, Reflective), IDEO-influenced teams create web products that build trust through visual calm, behavioral predictability, and reflective meaning. Research confirms that products perceived as emotionally safe feel easier to use, even when functionality is identical.

## How to implement
- Audit the product's visceral layer: first-impression UI elements — color palette, typography, spacing, empty states, loading screens — should signal competence, trustworthiness, and calm, not anxiety or unpredictability
- Design behavioral-level trust signals: consistent interaction patterns, predictable button behaviors, undo capabilities, explicit confirmation dialogs before irreversible actions, and clear system status indicators (as per Nielsen's heuristics)
- Craft microcopy that humanizes the experience: error messages that are empathetic and actionable ('Something went wrong — but your data is safe, try again'), onboarding copy that celebrates the user's first success, and transitions that acknowledge user effort
- Use storytelling as a trust-building mechanism: 'Share the Story' (IDEO's 7th design step) through case studies, user testimonials, and transparent communication about how the product works and why
- Design emotional safety for failure states: what happens when users make a mistake, lose data, or encounter an error should feel forgiving, not punishing

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: NPS, CSAT, Brand perception, Confidence rating
- **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Renewed subscriptions
- **Revenue**: Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), Conversion rate
**How to measure**: Use attitudinal surveys (NPS, CSAT) before and after design interventions that target emotional dimensions (trust signals, celebration moments, reassuring empty states). Track renewal rates and CLV cohorts to determine whether emotional design improvements drive long-term retention beyond short-term satisfaction gains.

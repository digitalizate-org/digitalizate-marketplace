## Why
IDEO uses a three-bucket research model — Generative (future opportunities and unmet needs), Evaluative (feedback on prototypes and experiments), and Validating (confirming what is currently true in the market) — to ensure product teams maintain a balanced understanding of past, present, and future user needs. As IDEO U explains, generative and evaluative research are future-facing and most valuable for innovation, while validating research confirms current reality. Most product teams over-rely on validating research (surveys, analytics) and under-invest in generative research, which is where breakthrough insights that lead to new features emerge.

## How to implement
- Audit your team's current research portfolio: count what percentage of research activities are generative (exploratory interviews, ethnography, diary studies), evaluative (usability testing, A/B tests, prototype feedback), and validating (surveys, NPS, market research)
- Deliberately plan at least one generative research activity per quarter — even 5 exploratory interviews with users can surface insight directions that quantitative tools cannot
- Use generative research when planning new features or entering new markets: conduct 'Jobs to be Done' interviews to understand what progress users are trying to make in their lives, independent of any existing product solution
- Use evaluative research iteratively throughout development: test paper prototypes with 5 users before wireframing, test wireframes before visual design, test visual designs before engineering build — each round catches a different class of problem
- Use validating research to confirm market assumptions before major investment: surveys, competitive analysis, and usage analytics validate whether a direction is worth pursuing at scale

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate, Error rate
- **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS
**How to measure**: Track which research type preceded each major design decision and correlate with downstream usability test scores. Compare post-launch support ticket volumes and task success rates between features informed by the appropriate research type (e.g., evaluative testing before release) versus those that skipped research phases.

## Why
IDEO incorporates behavioral design and habit formation science into its human-centered approach to product creation. Drawing on Charles Duhigg's habit loop (Cue → Routine → Reward) and behavioral science research, IDEO-influenced design teams create web products that integrate naturally into users' daily routines by establishing consistent contextual triggers, reducing friction in core actions, and providing immediate, meaningful rewards. Research from [IDEO.org](http://ideo.org/)'s educational courses shows that adding implementation intentions ('I will do X when Y happens') into onboarding significantly improves long-term retention.

## How to implement
- Map your product's core habit loop explicitly: identify the natural Cue (notification, time of day, external trigger), the Routine (the core action you want the user to take), and the Reward (what the user gets immediately upon completion)
- Reduce friction in the routine to near-zero: eliminate every unnecessary step between the cue and the reward — sign-up, modal interruptions, loading delays all break the habit loop
- Add 'implementation intention' prompts during onboarding: ask users to specify WHEN and WHERE they will use the product (e.g., 'I'll check this every morning before checking email') — this specificity dramatically improves habit formation
- Design rewards that feel immediate and intrinsic — progress bars, completion animations, streaks, and social acknowledgment all reinforce the routine; avoid hollow gamification that feels manipulative
- Use consistency of context as the primary habit driver: encourage users to use the product in the same place and time each day, rather than varying context

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Engagement & Usage**: Sessions per user, Active users, Session frequency
- **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Renewed subscriptions
- **Revenue**: Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), Revenue per user
**How to measure**: Define the intended trigger-routine-reward loop and instrument each stage with analytics (notification sent, action taken, reward displayed). Track daily and weekly active user rates before and after implementing habit-forming triggers, and measure 30-day retention curves for users who engage with the loop versus those who do not.

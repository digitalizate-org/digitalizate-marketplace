## Why
IDEO's DVF Framework, introduced in the early 2000s and popularized by Tim Brown's 'Change by Design,' is a three-lens decision model for product innovation: Desirability (do people actually want this?), Feasibility (can we build it with current technology and resources?), and Viability (does it make business sense?). The sweet spot where all three circles overlap is where successful web products live. This framework prevents teams from over-investing in technically clever features nobody wants, or building desirable features that cannot be sustained economically.

## How to implement
- Begin every feature proposal or product initiative by explicitly rating it across all three dimensions before any design or engineering work begins
- For Desirability: conduct generative research — user interviews, contextual observation, diary studies — to validate that a real human need exists; avoid relying solely on stakeholder assumptions
- For Feasibility: work with engineering to assess technical constraints, third-party dependencies, infrastructure needs, and realistic timelines given current team capacity
- For Viability: map out the revenue model, unit economics, and strategic alignment — ask whether this feature drives retention, conversion, or expansion revenue
- Use the framework as a structured design review template: present Desirability evidence, Feasibility constraints, and Viability projections to stakeholders before committing to full development
- Identify which lens is currently weakest and prioritize research or validation efforts to close that gap first
- Revisit the DVF assessment at each major milestone (alpha, beta, GA) as new evidence emerges

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Adoption & Retention**: Account creation rate, Renewed subscriptions
- **Revenue**: Conversion rate, Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS
**How to measure**: Evaluate products designed with the DVF framework against task success rates, conversion rates, and NPS across user segments; compare against products designed without explicit desirability-feasibility-viability alignment. Track long-term retention and CLV to assess whether DVF-guided products retain users who find them genuinely desirable.

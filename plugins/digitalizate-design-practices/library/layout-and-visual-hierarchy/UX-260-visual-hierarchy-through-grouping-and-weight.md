## Why
Use visual properties — proximity, size, weight, color, and whitespace — to create clear hierarchy that guides user attention to the most important information first, then secondary, then detail. Users scan before they read, and visual hierarchy determines what they notice and in what order. Data-heavy enterprise dashboards fail when they present all data at equal visual weight, forcing users to decide what matters rather than guiding them. Half the brain's resources are dedicated to visual processing; designs that work with visual processing shortcuts (proximity, similarity, pattern recognition) are processed faster and with less cognitive effort.

## How to implement
- Apply the 3-level hierarchy rule: primary elements at maximum contrast and size, secondary at medium weight, tertiary at minimum weight.
- Test hierarchy by squinting at the screen — can you identify the primary content at low resolution? Primary KPIs and action triggers should remain identifiable.
- Use consistent typographic scale (3–4 sizes maximum), consistent color weight (one primary action color, used sparingly), and whitespace grouping rather than borders.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Efficiency**: Time on task, Completion time
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Findability score
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, CSAT
**How to measure**: Use first-click testing to verify that users correctly identify primary and secondary content zones without prompting. Measure time-to-locate-target-element across grouped and ungrouped layout variants in unmoderated usability tests.

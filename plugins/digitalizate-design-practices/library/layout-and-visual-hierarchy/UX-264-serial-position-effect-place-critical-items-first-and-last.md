## Why
Users best remember the first and last items in a series (primacy and recency effects) — middle items are recalled with less accuracy. This applies to navigation (most important items at first and last positions), data tables (most important columns leftmost and rightmost), pricing tables (recommended plan first or last, never buried in the middle), and notification lists (most urgent at top). Applying this principle to navigation without analytics data leads to homepage bias — validate with actual usage data.

## How to implement
- Place the most important navigation items first (Home, Dashboard) and last (Settings, Help) in sidebars.
- Put the most important data table columns leftmost (primary identifier) and include a key status/action column rightmost.
- In pricing tables, place the recommended plan first or last — never bury it.
- Put critical form fields at the top of sections and a clear CTA at the bottom.
- In notification lists, ensure the most urgent items appear at the top.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Findability score
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Error counts
  - **Engagement & Usage**: Clickthrough rate, Bounce rate
  **How to measure**: Use eye-tracking studies or first-click testing to verify that critical items placed in primacy and recency positions receive the most attention and action. Compare clickthrough rates on navigation elements and CTAs before and after repositioning to serial-position-optimized locations.

## Why
Research by Cialdini and subsequent behavioral economists shows that scarcity created by social demand is significantly more powerful than scarcity by shortage alone — we want things most when we are in competition with others for them. Real-time activity signals ('5 people are viewing this right now', 'Sarah from Boston just joined') layer scarcity, social proof, and FOMO (fear of missing out) simultaneously, creating an urgency that is more motivating than static stock indicators. These signals are particularly effective because they are dynamic and personalized, triggering System 1's immediacy response. Authenticity is non-negotiable — fabricated signals are a legal and reputational risk.

## How to implement
- Implement real-time 'X users are viewing this' counters on high-value pages (pricing, limited availability products) using genuine session data
- Display recent activity notifications: 'John from Seattle just upgraded to Pro' with real data from recent conversions
- Show 'In-cart' indicators for limited inventory items ('15 people have this in their cart right now')
- Use 'Trending now' and 'Hot this week' labels on content and feature discovery surfaces backed by actual usage data
- Time display of social demand signals to appear when user intent is already high (they've returned to a page multiple times, engaged with pricing)
- Set minimum thresholds: only display social demand signals when they meet a truthful minimum (e.g., at least 3 active viewers) to maintain credibility

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Revenue**: Conversion rate, Average order value
- **Engagement & Usage**: Abandonment rate, Sessions per user
- **Adoption & Retention**: Signup completion rate
**How to measure**: A/B test real-time activity signals (e.g., "12 people viewing this now") against control pages; measure conversion rate, time-on-page, and abandonment rate; monitor whether FOMO signals create decision confidence or increase anxiety via post-purchase surveys.

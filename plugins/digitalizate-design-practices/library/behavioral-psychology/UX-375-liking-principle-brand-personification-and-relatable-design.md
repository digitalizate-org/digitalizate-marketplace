## Why
Cialdini's liking principle demonstrates that people are significantly more likely to be persuaded by, trust, and engage with people (and by extension, brands and products) they find likeable. Likeability is built through perceived similarity ('this brand gets people like me'), genuine compliments, and cooperative alignment with user goals. In product design, this translates to brand personality in copy and illustration, 'About Us' pages that humanize the team, and interfaces that celebrate users' successes with them. The Mailchimp brand (playful, warm, honest) and Basecamp's conversational copy are canonical examples of products that leverage liking to build deep user loyalty.

## How to implement
- Write all UI copy in a warm, human voice that reflects genuine care for user success — avoid corporate jargon and passive voice
- Include real team photos and authentic personal stories on About pages, creating human connection between users and the product
- Design UI to celebrate user achievements genuinely: congratulatory animations, milestone messages, and personalized success acknowledgments
- Use illustrations and avatars that reflect the diversity of your user base to increase perceived similarity
- Design error states and empty states with empathy and humor — how a product handles failure is the truest expression of its brand personality
- Personalize communications (onboarding emails, in-app messages) with user's name and context-specific references to their actual actions

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS, Brand perception, Satisfaction rating
  - **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Renewed subscriptions
  - **Revenue**: Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), Conversion rate
  **How to measure**: Measure brand affinity scores via NPS surveys before and after brand personification changes (e.g., introducing a mascot, conversational tone, or relatable UI language). Track long-term retention rates for users who rate the brand as relatable vs. neutral in post-onboarding surveys.

## Why
Thaler and Sunstein's nudge theory establishes that small, low-cost design interventions can predictably guide behavior without restricting freedom of choice. In digital products, every UI is a choice architecture — the layout, ordering, and presentation of options shapes decisions in ways users rarely consciously notice. Nudges work by appealing to System 1 (fast, automatic) thinking: they make the 'right' choice easier while preserving autonomy. Ethical nudges must be transparent, genuinely serve the user's interests, and always allow easy opt-out. The line between a nudge and a 'sludge' (manipulative friction) is whether the default benefits the user or exploits them.

## How to implement
- Place the most beneficial or recommended option in the most prominent visual position (first in a list, center of a tier comparison)
- Use visual weight — size, color contrast, borders — to draw attention to the recommended path without hiding alternatives
- Implement pre-filled form defaults and smart suggestions (e.g., suggested strong passwords, pre-filled common answers) to reduce effort
- Add timely micro-nudges: re-engagement emails that appear precisely when users are most ready to act (session lapse, trial expiry approaching)
- Create 'recommended' labels, progress indicators, and 'best for you' tags based on user context to guide without mandating
- Audit your choice architecture for sludge: unnecessary friction, confusing opt-outs, and buried alternatives are dark patterns

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Completion rate, Task success rate
- **Engagement & Usage**: Clickthrough rate, Abandonment rate
- **Revenue**: Conversion rate, Average order value
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, CSAT
**How to measure**: A/B test specific nudges — such as default opt-ins, ordering of options, or prominent calls-to-action — measuring conversion rate and completion rate lift. Use analytics to track clickthrough rates on nudged paths versus non-nudged alternatives, and conduct surveys to ensure nudges improve experience without feeling manipulative.

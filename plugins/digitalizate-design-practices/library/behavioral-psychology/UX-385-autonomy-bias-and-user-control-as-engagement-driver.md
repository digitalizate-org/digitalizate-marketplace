## Why
Autonomy bias describes the deep-seated human need to feel in control of one's environment and decisions. Coglode research identifies this as a core driver of product satisfaction: users who feel they have meaningful control over their experience report higher satisfaction and lower churn than those who feel the product is acting on them. Reactance theory (also from Cialdini) shows the inverse: when users feel their freedom is being restricted or they are being pushed too hard toward a specific action, they actively resist — even choices they would have made freely. This is the design principle that separates ethical nudges from dark patterns.

## How to implement
- Provide users with meaningful customization options for their workspace, notification preferences, and workflow — even small customizations increase engagement and ownership
- Offer genuine, easy-to-find opt-out options for all nudges, communications, and defaults — constrained choices feel manipulative even when users don't exercise the opt-out
- Use 'Choose your path' onboarding branching to let users self-select into the most relevant product experience
- Communicate the reasoning behind design choices when asking for permissions or data: 'We ask for location to show you local results — you can change this anytime'
- Design settings and preference screens with undo/redo capabilities — knowledge that actions are reversible dramatically lowers decision anxiety
- Avoid dark patterns (hidden unsubscribe, pre-checked opt-ins, forced account creation) — these trigger reactance and long-term brand damage that outweighs any short-term conversion gain

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Engagement & Usage**: Feature usage, Sessions per user, Active users
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS, Ease-of-use score
  - **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Renewed subscriptions
  **How to measure**: Track feature usage rates for customization and preference controls via analytics. Use post-task surveys to measure perceived control and satisfaction, and compare retention rates between user cohorts who actively personalize their experience vs. those who do not.

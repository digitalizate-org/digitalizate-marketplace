## Why
Tversky and Kahneman's framing research established that objectively identical information produces different decisions depending on how it is presented. '95% satisfaction rate' and '5% complaint rate' describe the same reality but produce measurably different emotional responses and behavioral outcomes. In product design, framing choices are present everywhere: how upgrade benefits are described, how error messages characterize problems, how risk is communicated. Positive framing (what users gain) encourages action when the gain is large; negative framing (what users lose) is more motivating for medium-value decisions because of loss aversion. Designers who understand framing can communicate the same truthful information in the way most likely to produce the desired, beneficial outcome.

## How to implement
- Default to gain framing for feature introductions and upgrades: 'Unlock 10x faster exports' rather than 'Stop waiting for slow exports'
- Apply loss framing for security, compliance, and risk communication where the stakes are high: 'Protect your data from breaches' outperforms 'Enable encryption'
- Use comparative framing on pricing pages: showing what competitors don't include makes your feature set feel more valuable
- Frame statistics as frequencies when communicating risk or performance: '1 in 20 users experience issues' is more viscerally impactful than '5%'
- Redesign error messages to be solution-oriented: 'Let's fix this — here's what happened and how to resolve it' rather than 'Error 403: Access Denied'
- A/B test framing variations systematically — small copy changes in framing can produce 10–30% conversion rate differences

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Conversion rate, Upgrade acceptance rate
- **Efficiency**: Decision time, User hesitation reduction
- **Revenue**: Feature upgrade conversion, Retention rate
**How to measure**: A/B test positive vs. negative framing variants on upgrade prompts and CTAs; track conversion rate differences between framing conditions; monitor error message clarity scores from support ticket reduction.

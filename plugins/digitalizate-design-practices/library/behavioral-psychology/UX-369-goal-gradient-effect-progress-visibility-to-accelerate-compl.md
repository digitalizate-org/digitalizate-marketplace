## Why
The goal gradient effect describes a psychological phenomenon where people's motivation increases as they perceive themselves getting closer to a goal. Originally demonstrated in rats by Hull (1934) and extended to humans by Nunes and Drèze (2006) through loyalty program research, this effect shows that progress visibility itself is a motivator — not just the reward at the end. In digital products, progress bars, onboarding checklists, completion percentages, and gamification streaks all leverage this effect. Critically, the effect activates only once a task is started, making it equally important to design for easy first-step engagement as for progression visibility.

## How to implement
- Add progress bars to all multi-step processes: onboarding, profile completion, form submission, and course progression
- Display profile or account 'completeness' scores that animate and update as users fill in more information (LinkedIn-style)
- Break large goals into milestone-based sub-goals so users experience the goal gradient effect multiple times within a single journey
- Implement artificial head-starts: Nunes and Drèze's research showed that a loyalty card with 2 pre-stamped stamps converts faster than an unstamped 10-stamp card — pre-fill early onboarding steps where possible
- Celebrate milestone achievements with micro-animations, badges, or congratulatory messages to reinforce progress
- Show remaining steps, not just completed ones, as users near the finish line to intensify the effect

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Completion rate, Task success rate
  - **Engagement & Usage**: Active users, Abandonment rate, Sessions per user
  - **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Renewed subscriptions
  **How to measure**: A/B test flows with and without visible progress indicators, measuring form or funnel completion rates. Track abandonment rate at each step to determine whether progress visibility reduces drop-off at high-friction steps.

## Why
Cialdini's scarcity principle establishes that people perceive scarce or time-limited options as more valuable, motivated by loss aversion — the fear of losing an opportunity forever. This is amplified when scarcity is driven by social demand ('Only 3 left — 12 people have this in their cart') rather than arbitrary limitation, triggering both scarcity and social proof simultaneously. [Booking.com](http://booking.com/) and e-commerce platforms have built entire conversion architectures around this principle. The mechanism is System 1 thinking: scarcity creates urgency that bypasses analytical deliberation. Ethical use requires that scarcity signals be accurate and not artificially manufactured.

## How to implement
- Display real inventory counts when stock is genuinely low ('Only 4 rooms left at this price')
- Use countdown timers for legitimately time-limited offers (seasonal sales, early-bird pricing)
- Show demand signals: 'X people are viewing this right now' or 'Sold to Y customers this week'
- Combine scarcity with social demand: 'Popular choice — only 2 remaining' layers both signals
- Apply to feature access or cohort-based products (limited beta seats, early access waiting lists)
- Never fabricate scarcity signals — users who detect false urgency experience reactance and permanent trust damage

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Revenue**: Conversion rate, Revenue per session
  - **Engagement & Usage**: Abandonment rate, Clickthrough rate, Active users
  - **Adoption & Retention**: Account creation rate, Signup completion rate
  **How to measure**: A/B test product pages with and without scarcity or urgency signals (limited availability, countdown timers), measuring conversion rate and time-to-decision. Monitor whether urgency signals reduce abandonment at key decision points without increasing post-purchase dissatisfaction (track CSAT and return rates).

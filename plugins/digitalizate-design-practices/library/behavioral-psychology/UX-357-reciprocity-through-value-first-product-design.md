## Why
Cialdini's reciprocity principle establishes that people feel a deep obligation to return favors when they have received something of value. In product design, offering genuine utility upfront — through generous free tiers, free educational content, or unprompted premium experiences — activates this hardwired social norm and significantly increases the likelihood of conversion and loyalty. Research from the Journal of Marketing Research indicates reciprocity-based strategies can increase conversion rates by up to 40% when the initial value feels authentic, not transactional. The key differentiator is that the gift must feel personalized and unexpected, not like a thinly veiled sales tactic.

## How to implement
- Lead with a free tier or trial that delivers real, standalone value (e.g., Dropbox's 2GB, HubSpot's free CRM) rather than a deliberately crippled preview
- Offer unexpected high-quality educational resources (guides, templates, courses) to free users without a paywall
- Surface premium feature value contextually when users encounter a need, not on a fixed cadence
- Ensure free users receive the same quality of customer support as paid users to maintain goodwill
- Avoid bait-and-switch patterns where free functionality is arbitrarily removed after the 'gift' is given
- Frame value delivery as genuinely user-centric, not as obligation-creation — users who detect manipulation resist rather than reciprocate

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Time-on-task
- **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Retention rate
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: NPS, CSAT
**How to measure**: Track retention cohorts segmented by onboarding flow variant. Users acquired through value-first flows retain longer.

## Why
Kahneman's peak-end rule establishes that people judge an experience almost entirely by its most intense moment (the 'peak', positive or negative) and its ending, with minimal weight given to the duration or average quality of the experience. This 'duration neglect' has profound implications for product design: a user's decision to return, recommend, or pay for a product is driven by the one or two moments that stood out and how the session ended — not by the overall experience quality. Mailchimp's famous animated high-five after campaign launch is a textbook positive peak. Uber's design effort to manage waiting-time perception targets the negative peak.

## How to implement
- Map the user journey to identify the current emotional peak moments and session endings — are they positive or negative?
- Deliberately design 1–2 'wow' moments at high-value milestones: first successful action, first value realization, first collaborative result
- Invest disproportionately in offboarding and session-end states: confirmation screens, completion animations, and summary recaps create positive final impressions
- Remove or mitigate known negative peaks: long waits, error states, and confusing decision points are disproportionately damaging to recall
- Apply the rule to micro-sessions too: the end of a support interaction, the last screen of an onboarding flow, the final step of a checkout
- Design negative-peak mitigation: humor during wait states, proactive error messaging, and graceful degradation convert potential pain spikes into neutral or positive memories

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS, Satisfaction rating, Brand perception
  - **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Renewed subscriptions
  - **Engagement & Usage**: Sessions per user, Active users
  **How to measure**: Use post-session microsurveys immediately after peak moments (e.g., task completion, milestone achievement) to capture in-the-moment satisfaction. Compare NPS scores for sessions that include engineered peak moments vs. sessions that do not, controlling for task type and outcome.

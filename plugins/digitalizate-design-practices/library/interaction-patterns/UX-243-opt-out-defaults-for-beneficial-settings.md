## Why
Opt-out defaults achieve higher participation rates than opt-in because they leverage the implication of a recommended state — the default is implicitly the suggested choice. Applied ethically (not exploitatively), this approach uses behavioral design to maximize adoption of features that genuinely benefit users. The critical ethical constraint: exploitative opt-outs that benefit the company at user expense violate GDPR and erode trust permanently.

## How to implement
- Default new accounts to the most sensible notification and preference settings with clear disclosure of what they're opted into and an easy path to change.
- For data-sharing and analytics, be transparent and make opt-out genuinely accessible — exploitative opt-outs violate GDPR and user trust.
- In configuration wizards, pre-select recommended settings (clearly labeled as recommendations) rather than requiring active configuration from zero.
- Apply only where the default genuinely benefits the user, not where it benefits the company at user expense.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Adoption & Retention**: Signup completion rate, Renewed subscriptions
- **Engagement & Usage**: Feature usage, Active users
- **Revenue**: Conversion rate
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, Satisfaction rating
**How to measure**: Compare feature activation rates for settings that use opt-out defaults versus opt-in defaults via analytics; track long-term engagement and retention for cohorts onboarded with beneficial opt-out defaults versus those who had to manually enable them.

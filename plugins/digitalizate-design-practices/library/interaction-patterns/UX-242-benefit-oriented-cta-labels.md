## Why
Buttons that reinforce a benefit ("Get Your Report," "Export to Excel") outperform purely task-based buttons ("Submit," "Continue"). The benefit reminds users why they're taking the action, reducing friction at the commitment moment. For recurring workflow CTAs, generic labels like "Submit" provide no confirmation of what the action will produce. Every CTA label is an opportunity to set accurate expectations and reinforce value.

## How to implement
- Replace generic "Submit" with action-outcome labels: "Create My Report," "Export to Excel," "Send Proposal." Near upgrade or upsell prompts, pair the CTA with a one-line benefit statement: "Upgrade to Pro → Unlock unlimited exports." For recurring workflow CTAs (like "Run Analysis"), make the label descriptive of the output, not just the action.
- Test benefit-oriented labels against task-oriented alternatives — they consistently outperform in A/B studies.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Revenue**: Conversion rate, Revenue per session
  - **Engagement & Usage**: Clickthrough rate, Active users, Abandonment rate
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, Ease-of-use score
  **How to measure**: A/B test benefit-oriented CTA labels (e.g., "Save 20% Now") against generic labels (e.g., "Continue"), measuring clickthrough rate and downstream conversion rate. Use heatmaps to confirm that relabeled CTAs attract more interaction relative to surrounding elements.

## Why
Google's HEART framework quantifies user experience quality across five dimensions: Happiness (user satisfaction, measured via CSAT or NPS), Engagement (frequency and depth of use), Adoption (new user uptake of features), Retention (continued use over time), and Task Success (efficiency and error rates). This framework bridges the gap between UX work and business metrics, allowing product teams to set measurable UX goals and demonstrate product quality in quantitative terms that resonate with business stakeholders.

## How to implement
- For each product area or major feature, define one metric per HEART dimension.
- Set baseline measurements and improvement targets.
- For Task Success: measure time-on-task and error rate via session recording analysis.
- For Happiness: deploy regular CSAT surveys at task completion points.
- For Engagement: track weekly active users and feature usage frequency via analytics.
- Report HEART metrics alongside functional KPIs in all product reviews.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Error rate
- **Adoption & Retention**: Feature adoption rate, Daily active users
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Ease-of-use score, NPS
**How to measure**: Survey users on whether they feel progress toward a concrete user goal rather than a vanity indicator.

## Why
Begin feature design by deciding how much time a problem is worth solving — the "appetite" — rather than estimating how long a solution will take. This reverses conventional planning logic: instead of building what users want and hoping it fits the timeline, you decide the budget first and design a solution that fits within it. For complex enterprise features, appetite setting forces explicit trade-offs upfront rather than discovering scope creep mid-build when switching costs are highest. The appetite is a constraint that shapes the solution, not a prediction.

## How to implement
- Define appetite as "small batch" (1–2 weeks) or "big batch" (up to 6 weeks) before any design work begins.
- Ask: "How much is it worth investing in this?" not "How long will it take?" Design the solution to fit the appetite.
- If the simplest viable solution requires more time than the appetite allows, question whether a reduced-scope version is acceptable or whether the problem should be deferred.
- Document appetite explicitly in the feature brief.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Completion rate, Task success rate
- **Efficiency**: Time saved per task, Completion time
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Satisfaction rating, CSAT
**How to measure**: Compare feature delivery timelines and post-release usability benchmarks for projects scoped with appetite-setting versus unconstrained projects. Survey product teams and stakeholders on satisfaction with delivered scope and perceived quality against time invested.

## Why
Apply OKRs (Objectives and Key Results) to product quality and UX goals, not just business outcomes. Setting explicit OKRs for user experience — "Reduce time to complete primary task by 40%," "Achieve 80% user satisfaction with data entry workflow" — gives design work measurable targets and creates organizational accountability for UX investment. Without explicit quality OKRs, design quality is perpetually subordinated to feature velocity because velocity is always measured and quality often isn't.

## How to implement
- For each product area, set one Objective focused on user experience quality alongside any business Objectives.
- Define 2–3 Key Results that are measurable via available instrumentation (task completion rate, time-on-task, error rate, CSAT score, NPS).
- Baseline current performance before setting targets.
- Track quarterly and include in cycle reviews alongside feature delivery metrics.
- When quality OKRs are missed, investigate with user research to understand root causes before designing solutions.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS, Ease-of-use score
  **How to measure**: Define design quality OKRs tied to measurable UX metrics (e.g., "Improve task success rate from 72% to 85% by Q3") and track progress quarterly via usability benchmarking. Use OKR retrospectives to assess whether design quality goals correlate with product-level business metric improvements.

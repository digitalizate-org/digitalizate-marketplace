## Why
Establish a lightweight continuous research practice — weekly or bi-weekly user interviews, monthly survey cadence, ongoing session recording review — rather than large periodic research projects. Continuous research provides a constant pulse on user needs and enables product decisions to be informed by fresh evidence rather than months-old findings. Even 2 user interviews per week at 30 minutes each — 4 hours of researcher time — provides dramatically better product decisions than no regular research. The cost of not doing continuous research is compounded by each poorly informed feature decision.

## How to implement
- Schedule standing research slots (e.g., Tuesday 10 AM = 2 user interviews, rotating topics each week).
- Build a participant recruitment pipeline with a standing list of willing research participants.
- Share 3-sentence research summaries in the weekly product team meeting.
- Maintain a living research repository (Notion, Confluence, Dovetail) that all team members can search and access.
- Rotate who observes research sessions to build empathy across the team.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
  - **Efficiency**: Time on task, Support tickets reduction
  - **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Account creation rate
  **How to measure**: Track product metrics week-over-week to detect regression or improvement correlated with research-driven design changes. Use a research operations log to tie specific findings to measurable metric shifts over each quarter.

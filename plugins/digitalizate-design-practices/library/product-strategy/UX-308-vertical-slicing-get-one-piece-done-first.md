## Why
In the first days of a building cycle, complete one fully integrated vertical slice of the product — front-end, back-end, and interaction — rather than building horizontal layers (all back-end first, then all front-end). A completed vertical slice creates something demoable and testable early, validates that the overall approach works, and builds team momentum. Horizontal layering produces a large volume of work with nothing "really done" until the last day, creating invisible risk and delayed feedback on whether the technical approach is sound.

## How to implement
- Before building anything, identify the one slice that is (1) core to the project's purpose, (2) completable end-to-end in 2–3 days, and (3) novel enough to validate the important unknowns.
- Build it completely — front-end, back-end, integration.
- Demo it internally.
- Use the feedback from this vertical slice to calibrate the team's understanding of the remaining work's complexity.
- Only then expand to secondary slices.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Efficiency**: Completion time, Time saved per task
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Satisfaction rating, Confidence rating
**How to measure**: Compare usability test results and user satisfaction scores for features shipped via vertical slicing versus those shipped all at once; track the time from start to first usable feature delivery. Monitor abandonment and error rates on the initial vertical slice to validate the core experience before expanding.

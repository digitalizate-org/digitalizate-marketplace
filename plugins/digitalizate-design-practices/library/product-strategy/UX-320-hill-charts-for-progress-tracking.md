## Why
Track project scope progress using a hill chart metaphor: the uphill slope represents work that is still uncertain (figuring out the approach), the hilltop represents full clarity on remaining work, and the downhill slope represents confident execution of known work. Plotting each project scope as a dot on the hill communicates the nature of remaining work — not just quantity — and makes invisible uncertainty visible to managers and stakeholders without requiring status meetings. A scope stuck on the uphill side for multiple days signals a rabbit hole or blocker requiring immediate attention.

## How to implement
- Plot each project scope as a named dot on the hill.
- Update weekly or more frequently for fast-moving cycles.
- A scope that hasn't moved uphill in 2+ days should trigger a conversation: "What would move this scope over the hill?" (not "When will it be done?").
- When a scope appears stuck, examine whether it conflates both uphill (uncertain) and downhill (execution) work — if so, split it into two separate scopes that can move independently.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Completion rate
- **Efficiency**: Completion time, Time saved per task
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: Confidence rating, Satisfaction rating
**How to measure**: Track the accuracy of team delivery estimates for projects managed with hill charts versus projects without this visibility tool, comparing planned versus actual completion timelines. Survey stakeholders and team members on confidence in project progress using pre/post ratings when hill charts are introduced.

## Why
External rewards (gamification points, achievement badges) can undermine intrinsic motivation for complex professional tasks. Enterprise users are motivated by doing their job well — by competence, autonomy, and meaningful work — not by accumulating points. Adding extrinsic reward systems to professional tools can cheapen the experience and signal organizational distrust of users' professional motivation. Research shows that introducing extrinsic rewards for tasks users already find intrinsically rewarding can reduce their subsequent intrinsic engagement with those tasks.

## How to implement
- Evaluate every gamification proposal against the intrinsic motivation test: does this feature help users feel more competent, autonomous, or purposeful? Progress visualization (showing users they've gotten faster, made fewer errors, or completed more work) is intrinsically motivating.
- Leaderboards comparing individual performance are typically not.
- Invest design energy in features that increase task efficiency, reduce errors, and save time — these address intrinsic motivators directly.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: NPS, Ease-of-use score, Satisfaction rating
- **Engagement & Usage**: Feature usage, Active users, Session frequency
- **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Renewed subscriptions
**How to measure**: Survey users on intrinsic motivation factors (autonomy, mastery, purpose) at regular intervals via NPS and in-app surveys; track feature engagement depth and DAU/WAU ratios to distinguish intrinsic-engagement patterns from extrinsic reward-chasing.

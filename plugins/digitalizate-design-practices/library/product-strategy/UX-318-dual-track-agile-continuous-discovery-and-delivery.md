## Why
Organize product work into two parallel tracks: Discovery (validating ideas, conducting user research, designing solutions) and Delivery (implementing validated solutions). Discovery track produces validated product ideas running 1–2 cycles ahead of the Delivery track. This separation ensures the Delivery track never starts on unvalidated work, while Discovery never feels pressure to rush because builders are waiting. Teams that run both tracks in parallel ship faster with fewer post-launch revisions than teams that run them sequentially.

## How to implement
- Designate specific team members as the Discovery track (senior designer, PM) who do not participate in Delivery during active cycles.
- The Discovery output for each cycle is a set of validated, shaped concepts ready to commit to.
- Delivery takes these and implements them without needing to revisit fundamental problem or solution validation.
- Conduct joint weekly syncs between tracks to maintain alignment and surface new information from Delivery that should inform Discovery.

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Effectiveness**: Task success rate, Completion rate
- **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Renewed subscriptions
- **Revenue**: Conversion rate, Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS
**How to measure**: Track the ratio of features shipped that address validated user needs versus unvalidated assumptions across dual-track sprints. Monitor CSAT and NPS trends over release cycles to assess whether continuous discovery correlates with improved satisfaction, and use A/B tests to evaluate specific discovery-informed feature changes.

## Why
Points, Badges, and Leaderboards (PBL) are the most commonly implemented gamification mechanics, but they form only a 'shell' of a game experience — adding extrinsic rewards to boring tasks rarely makes them engaging. Research consistently shows that once extrinsic motivators are removed, user motivation drops below baseline. True gamification success comes from designing for the underlying motivational core: why users want to act, feel, and return, not merely what mechanics are visible.

## How to implement
- Before choosing mechanics, ask: 'How do I want users to feel?' — proud, excited, part of something bigger, creative — then choose mechanics that produce those feelings
- Ensure core tasks have genuine value; gamification amplifies value, it cannot create it
- Use PBL mechanics only where they serve intrinsic goals: badges for genuine skill milestones, leaderboards only where competition is culturally appropriate
- Test whether removing points/badges would reduce engagement — if users only act for rewards, the core experience is weak
- Design for at least one intrinsic reward per key interaction: mastery satisfaction, creative expression, meaningful contribution, or social connection
- Avoid gamification in contexts where it trivializes the task (medical, legal, sensitive financial decisions)
- Measure engagement quality (feature depth, session value) not just quantity (logins, clicks) to detect hollow PBL-driven metrics

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
- **Satisfaction & Perception**: NPS, Satisfaction rating
- **Engagement & Usage**: Feature usage, Sessions per user, Active users
- **Adoption & Retention**: Renewed subscriptions, Returning visitors
**How to measure**: Survey users to distinguish intrinsic versus extrinsic engagement drivers; track engagement depth (features used, tasks completed) and 90-day retention rates, and compare cohorts with intrinsically designed flows versus badge-heavy flows.

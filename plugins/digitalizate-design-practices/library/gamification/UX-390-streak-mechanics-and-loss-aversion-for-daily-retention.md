## Why
Streak systems leverage loss aversion — people feel losses approximately 2x more intensely than equivalent gains — to drive daily return behavior. Duolingo increased retention from 12% to 55% through streak design; Snapchat's streak system makes users feel shared social responsibility to maintain consecutive-day interactions. The longer a streak grows, the more powerful the loss aversion becomes, creating a compounding retention flywheel.

## How to implement
- Define a single, achievable daily action as the streak trigger (e.g., 'complete one lesson', 'log one meal', 'make one commit')
- Display streak length prominently with a visual symbol (flame, chain, calendar) and current day count
- Implement 'Streak Freeze' or grace period mechanics — Duolingo's streak freeze feature reduced churn by 21% by allowing occasional misses without loss
- Send push/email reminders at the user's habitual action time (not a fixed time for all users) when streak is at risk
- Celebrate streak milestones visibly (7-day, 30-day, 100-day) with disproportionate recognition — social sharing amplifies this
- Combine streaks with milestones: streaks measure consistency, milestones measure cumulative progress — both are needed
- Avoid punishing users who break streaks without redemption paths — offer 'streak repair' options to prevent permanent churn after a miss

## Metrics
Applying this pattern can positively influence the following UX metrics:
  - **Adoption & Retention**: Returning visitors, Renewed subscriptions, Session frequency
  - **Engagement & Usage**: Active users, Sessions per user, Abandonment rate
  - **Satisfaction & Perception**: CSAT, NPS, Satisfaction rating
  **How to measure**: Track daily active user (DAU) rates before and after streak implementation using product analytics. Monitor churn rate for users who lose a streak vs. those who maintain it to quantify the loss-aversion retention effect.
